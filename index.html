04 / TEACHING

Mathematics as a
scientific language.

Applied mathematics, from theory to computational research.

My research asks how the structure of a dynamical system constrains its behaviour. I bring that question into my courses in mathematical modelling and reaction network analysis at Friedrich Schiller University Jena, where students connect the algebra of a network to the dynamics of its solutions. My teaching across Jena, Nuremberg and Weihenstephan-Triesdorf spans more than 15 years.

Selected courses

  • Mathematical Modelling and Reaction Network Analysis (Friedrich Schiller University Jena)
  • Analysis I, II and III
  • Linear Algebra
  • Ordinary and Partial Differential Equations
  • Probability and Statistics
  • Optimisation
  • Programming and Simulation with MATLAB/Simulink
  • Scientific Computing (English-language master's course)

Research-led teaching

I also teach the mathematics at the heart of my Diplom and doctoral research: chemical organisation theory, dynamical systems and reaction-diffusion models. One question connects these subjects: what can the structure of a reaction network tell us about which species can persist over time? I have developed this work in research talks and conference contributions, including my 2019 World Conference on Complex Systems contribution on reaction-diffusion systems. My invited talks include a workshop at the University of Granada in 2022.

Supervision and student research

I have supervised more than 50 bachelor's and master's theses and co-supervised doctoral research in chemical organisation theory. My students have worked on problems in engineering, bioinformatics, sustainability and data analysis, often with industry partners. My collaboration with Linus Woitke led to a joint first-author Scientific Reports paper on computing all persistent subspaces of a reaction-diffusion system.
